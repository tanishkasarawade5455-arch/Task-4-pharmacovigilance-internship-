# Task-4-pharmacovigilance-internship-
Severity &amp; Case Classification
Introduction
This report presents the assessment of adverse drug reaction (ADR) cases according to ICH E2A regulatory guidance. Each case was evaluated for seriousness based on standard criteria including death, life-threatening event, hospitalization, disability/incapacity, congenital anomaly, and other medically important conditions. Expectedness was assessed against the reference safety information. Regulatory reporting requirements were assigned accordingly.

---

Case SYN-PV-005
The patient experienced tendon pain following administration of ciprofloxacin. The event was classified as a medically important condition. According to ICH E2A criteria, this case is considered Serious. The reaction is listed in the reference safety information and is therefore Expected. Expedited regulatory reporting within 15 calendar days is applicable. ￼

Case SYN-PV-006
The patient experienced dizziness after treatment with sertraline. The event was reported as medically important. Therefore, the case is classified as Serious. The reaction is expected according to the reference safety information. Expedited reporting within 15 calendar days is required. 
￼
Case SYN-PV-007
Headache was reported following omeprazole administration. The event was categorized as medically important and thus meets the definition of a Serious adverse event. The reaction is expected based on product safety information. Regulatory reporting timeline is 15 calendar days. 
￼
Case SYN-PV-008
Fatigue was reported with losartan use. The case was recorded as Not Serious and does not meet any ICH E2A seriousness criteria. Although the reaction is unexpected according to the reference data, expedited reporting is not required. The case should be included in periodic safety reporting activities. 
￼
Case SYN-PV-009
Rash occurred following amoxicillin treatment and was considered medically important. The case is classified as Serious and Expected. A 15-day expedited report is applicable. ￼

Cases SYN-PV-010, SYN-PV-014, SYN-PV-015, SYN-PV-018, SYN-PV-020 and SYN-PV-023
These cases involved events resulting in hospitalization. Hospitalization is an explicit seriousness criterion under ICH E2A; therefore, all cases are classified as Serious. The associated reactions were expected according to the reference safety information. Each case requires 15-day expedited reporting. ￼

Cases SYN-PV-011, SYN-PV-012, SYN-PV-013, SYN-PV-017, SYN-PV-021, SYN-PV-025, SYN-PV-026, SYN-PV-027 and SYN-PV-029
These cases were categorized as Medically Important Events. Under ICH E2A, medically important events meet the definition of seriousness even when they do not result in hospitalization or disability. All cases are therefore classified as Serious. The reactions were expected according to reference safety information and require 15-day expedited reporting. ￼

Cases SYN-PV-016, SYN-PV-019, SYN-PV-022 and SYN-PV-028
These events were explicitly reported as Not Serious and do not fulfill any regulatory seriousness criteria. Consequently, they are classified as Non-Serious. Expedited reporting is not required, and these cases should be handled through routine periodic safety reporting. ￼

Case SYN-PV-024
Fatigue associated with losartan resulted in hospitalization. Because hospitalization occurred, the case is classified as Serious. The reaction is identified as Unexpected according to the reference safety information. This represents the only Serious Unexpected case in the dataset and qualifies for expedited reporting within 7-15 calendar days, consistent with ICH E2A requirements. ￼

---

Overall Assessment Summary
A total of 25 ADR cases were evaluated. Most cases met seriousness criteria due to hospitalization or classification as medically important events and were therefore considered Serious. A smaller number of cases were assessed as Non-Serious and do not require expedited reporting. One case (SYN-PV-024) was identified as a Serious Unexpected Adverse Drug Reaction, requiring the most urgent regulatory reporting. All remaining serious expected cases require expedited reporting within 15 calendar days, while non-serious cases should be managed through routine periodic safety reporting. ￼
